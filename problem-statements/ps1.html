09

Reflection & conclusion

For me, PS1 is really about construction readiness. Nothing on this site can start well until three things line up: the approvals and NOCs that allow work to begin, a layout that lets material and people move without crossing, and a duration estimate realistic enough to plan around.

Observations

  • The approvals list (local body, RERA, Fire NOC, pollution control, HESCOM, water and sewerage, labour / OSH) is effectively the first critical path — it sits before any physical work.
  • Putting the store and lab at Gate 1 and visitors at Gate 2 means the busiest movement on site, material entry, never meets the sales traffic.
  • Keeping RMC as the base option while reserving a plant pad keeps the decision open without costing layout space later.
  • The ~10% monsoon allowance (440 → ≈ 485 days) is the deck's way of admitting weather risk instead of hiding it.

Key takeaways

  • Sequence approvals before mobilisation, not alongside it.
  • A layout is a logistics plan, not just a drawing.
  • Treat proportional allocations and indicative areas as provisional until a take-off replaces them.

Team conclusion retained: approvals and NOCs, RMC as base supply with a reserve pad, a one-way two-gate layout and ≈ 485 days (18–19 months). Allocations are assumptions, areas indicative and the layout schematic.
