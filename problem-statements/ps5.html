Arithmetic checks

  • 12,000 m³ × 110 kg/m³ = 1,320 t ✓; 1,320,000 ÷ 200 = 6,600 man-days ✓

Review flag — Helpers are described as '1 per 2 skilled'. Half of all skilled man-days (6,600 + 9,330 + 2,700 = 18,630) would be 9,315; the table's 7,965 equals half of fabricator + carpenter man-days only (15,930 ÷ 2). The table value is kept — confirm the intended basis.

04

Managing the curve

Too few vs too many

  • Too few: pours and cycles slip
  • Too many: idle workers, cost and congestion

Controls proposed

  • Wave sequencing across the seven blocks
  • Core labour team plus a flexible gang for peak pours
  • Cross-training carpenters and masons
  • Weekly productivity review; adjust labour a month ahead

07

Reflection & conclusion

For me, PS5 is about matching labour to what the work actually needs at each stage. Too few workers stall pours; too many sit idle and crowd a site that is already constrained. The numbers give a shape to plan around — but they are only as good as the assumptions underneath them.

Observations

  • The histogram shows the real peak in basement and superstructure work (80 skilled + 75 unskilled at superstructure), with the concrete gang shrinking in finishing.
  • Peak ÷ average = 155 ÷ 92 ≈ 1.68, inside the deck's 1.5–1.7 range — the estimate is internally consistent.
  • Several inputs (110 kg/m³ steel, 7 m²/m³ formwork, area-based concrete per block) are assumptions that a take-off and site productivity records should replace.
  • The helper basis (7,965 man-days) needs confirming before it is used for hiring.

Key takeaways

  • Plan labour as a curve with a core team and a flexible peak gang.
  • Review productivity weekly and adjust a month ahead.
  • Re-test assumptions and verify wage notifications before contracts are signed.

Team conclusion retained: average 92 and peak 155 workers (skilled peak 80, unskilled peak 75), managed through wave sequencing, cross-training and monthly look-ahead. Estimates are indicative.
