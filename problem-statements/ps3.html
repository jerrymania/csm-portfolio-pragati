Surveying Plan & Work Management System

Dividing survey work between a specialist consultant and the in-house team — and preparing so that two planned visits are enough.

Problem and context

Execution starts in 5 days. A Bengaluru consultant brings a total station to Hubballi to establish permanent benchmarks and mark RLs, charging ₹10,000 per visit plus travel and stay. The aim is to minimise visits and explain why qualified civil engineers may still outsource surveying. Source

05

Why outsource surveying

Equipment cost

A total station is costly to buy and maintain for a few uses.

Specialist skill

Traversing and control work need a practised operator.

Engineer focus

Site engineers stay on execution.

Independent proof

Third-party control is an independent record.

Accuracy / liability

The surveyor carries responsibility for the control.

Time pressure

Five days to start leaves no time to train.

07

Reflection & conclusion

PS3 made me think about where specialist skill is genuinely needed and where an in-house team is enough. The consultant's value is in the control — benchmarks, grids, key RLs — while day-to-day levelling from those benchmarks is well within the site engineers' reach.

Observations

  • Most of the saving comes from the Day 0–4 preparation: the consultant's visit is only as useful as the drawings, coordinates and access ready for it.
  • The benchmark register is the hand-over point between the two teams.
  • The request form turns an unplanned visit into a decision someone has to justify.
  • The ₹1,00,000 figure depends on the assumed ₹15,000 travel and stay — it shows the scale of the effect, not a guaranteed saving.

Key takeaways

  • Outsource the control, keep the routine.
  • Plan visits around milestones (Day 5, plinth), not problems.
  • Independent control is also independent evidence.

Team conclusion retained: two planned visits (Day 5 and plinth) with an optional third only on request, in-house levelling between visits, and an illustrative saving of about ₹1,00,000.
